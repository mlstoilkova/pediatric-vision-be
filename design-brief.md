VII. Design Guidelines

I already have an approved visual identity that includes:
- Logo – attached, name of the file: pediatric-vision-logo.svg
- color palette – attached, name of the file: color-palette.svg
- font – Poppins
- custom playful illustrated characters – attached with names that start with “character” or “element”

I attached these assets. Please use them as the foundation of the website design and do not redesign or replace them. Subtle animations of the characters and elements are welcome. 

The primary audience is parents looking for pediatric ophthalmology care for their children. The website should feel trustworthy, professional and medically credible, while also being warm, friendly and reassuring for children.

The desired visual balance is:
professional + warm + clean + playful + contemporary

The playful feeling should come mainly from the custom characters, colors and selected visual details. The underlying interface should remain structured, calm and easy to navigate.

Avoid:
- generic hospital website aesthetics
- excessive medical-blue styling
- overly childish layouts
- cartoon-heavy interfaces
- rainbow color schemes
- excessive decoration
- generic stock-website appearance
- large walls of text
- repetitive section layouts

The doctor has already provided a lot of content. Do not treat the existing content structure as the website structure.

I want you to act as a senior UI/UX designer and design the information.

Please:
analyze the supplied content
- identify the most important information for parents
- group related information
- create a clear information hierarchy
- decide what belongs on the homepage and what should move to secondary pages
- propose an appropriate navigation structure
- use different section compositions where appropriate
- create a clear path toward booking an appointment
- integrate the illustrated characters intentionally rather than simply decorating empty areas

Important information that should be easy to understand includes:
- who the doctor is
- her pediatric ophthalmology specialization
- the services and conditions she works with
- how a pediatric eye examination works
- useful information for parents
- the three consultation locations
- how to book an appointment

Responsive design

Design for desktop and mobile from the beginning.

Do not simply shrink the desktop design for mobile.

On mobile:
prioritize information hierarchy
- maintain comfortable reading sizes
- simplify layouts where necessary
- reposition or reduce decorative illustrations when needed
- maintain clear and accessible calls to action

Accessibility
Keep accessibility in mind throughout the design:
- good color contrast
- readable typography
- clear hierarchy
- large enough touch targets
- simple navigation
- clear buttons and links

First task
Do not design the entire website immediately.

Start by:
1. Reviewing the supplied brand assets and content
2. Proposing the information architecture
3. Proposing the main navigation
4. Proposing the homepage section structure
5. Briefly explaining the reasoning behind that structure

After establishing the structure, create the first homepage design direction using the approved brand identity.

Do not introduce a new visual identity.

Where a design decision is ambiguous, prefer clarity, whitespace and simplicity over adding more elements.
