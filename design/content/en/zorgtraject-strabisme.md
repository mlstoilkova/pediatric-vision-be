Strabismus care pathway

Strabismus (squint) is an abnormal position of the eyes in which the two eyes are not aimed at the same point. Strabismus develops in 5% of children; in adults the frequency is 4%. Risk factors are heredity, refractive errors, amblyopia, neurological conditions, endocrine conditions and trauma.

(Alarm) Symptoms of strabismus:
- abnormal eye position
- squinting or closing one eye
- abnormal head posture
- double vision in older children and adults
- Asthenopia – headaches after visual work, difficulty reading, complaints of fluctuating blurred vision

Stages of the care pathway:
- Detection: parents, Kind en Gezin, CLB, GPs, screening because of a family risk of strabismus
- Referral:
Urgent (within 2 weeks) for sudden-onset strabismus, double vision or neurological symptoms

📢 Red flags: vomiting with esotropia, constant strabismus in a baby under 6 months with poor eye contact or an abnormal reflex in the deviating eye, sudden-onset strabismus with neurological symptoms
Semi-urgent: risk of amblyopia, Graves’ disease, intermittent strabismus becoming constant
Not urgent: long-standing strabismus

Examination methods
Eye motility is examined together with an orthoptist:
Eye position is assessed with the cover test and the alternating cover–uncover test.
Binocular eye movements: pursuit and saccadic movements, convergence and divergence.
Ocular dominance is assessed with a prism cover test.
The deviation is measured with a prism test and a synoptophore examination.
Stereo vision is measured with the Lang test.

Hess screen: a more advanced test, often used in adult patients with double vision. The Hess screen helps in the differential diagnosis of paralytic, restrictive or comitant strabismus.

Cycloplegic refraction: Cyclogyl 0.5% or Cyclogyl 1%. Cycloplegia sets in 30 minutes after the drops are instilled. Only then can the cycloplegic refraction be measured.

Treatment of strabismus
- Glasses
- Occlusion (patching)
- Prism prescription
- Surgery
- Botulinum toxin injection

Timely detection (red flags) → referral to a strabismus consultation → diagnosis → treatment → follow-up.
