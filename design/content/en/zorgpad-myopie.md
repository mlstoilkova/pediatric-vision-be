Myopia care pathway

Target group: children aged 4–18

Signs:
- Squinting, sitting close to the TV or holding books close.
- Complaints of blurred distance vision (the board at school).
- Headaches or eye strain when reading

Risk factors:
- Parents with myopia.
- Asian descent
- A lot of near work and screen use: tablets, smartphones, intensive reading

Referral to an ophthalmologist/orthoptist
Indications for referral:
- Visual complaints or suspected myopia.
- Progression of an existing prescription.
- Risk group (parents with high myopia, children under 10 who already have myopia).

Diagnostics
- Cycloplegic refraction
- Axial length measurement
- Fundus examination

📢 Red flags
Myopia in children under 2 years
Myopia with associated complaints: watery eye, painful eye
- Associated atypical visual complaints and symptoms: nystagmus
- Reduced vision in the dark (always switches the light on…)
- Very sensitive to light
- Often bumping into things when moving around
- Hyperlaxity, hearing loss in the child or the family, heart problems, tall stature with a large arm span

Treatment options for progressive myopia
- Prevention through lifestyle advice:
more than 2 hours outdoors every day, limiting screen use, taking breaks, keeping enough distance when reading and using screens.
- Optical interventions:
  > Myopia-control spectacle lenses (DIMS, HAL).
  > MiyoSmart contact lenses (for children over 12)
- Medication:
low-dose atropine eye drops (0.02–0.05%).
